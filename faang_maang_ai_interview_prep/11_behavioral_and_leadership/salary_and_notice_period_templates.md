# Salary & Notice Period Conversation Templates

> **Type:** Template — copy or fill in directly

> **Varies:** Compensation negotiation norms, ranges, and what's disclosable vary heavily by
> company, location, and local pay-transparency law — always check the actual regulations for
> your jurisdiction, this file is a communication template, not compensation advice.

## Handling "What are your salary expectations?" early in the process

Goal: avoid anchoring low, avoid refusing to engage (which can read as evasive), and avoid a
number before you have enough information (level, location, full comp structure).

```markdown
**Template response:**
"I'd like to learn more about the role and level before landing on a specific number — could you
share the compensation range for this position/level? That'll help me give you a number that's
actually useful rather than a guess."
```

If pushed for a number anyway:

```markdown
"Based on my experience and market research for [role] at [level] in [location], I'm targeting
[range, not a single number] — but I'm flexible depending on the full package and the scope of
the role."
```

## Handling a lowball or below-expectation offer

```markdown
"Thank you for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about the team and the role. The base
compensation is a bit below where I'd hoped to land given [specific market data / competing offer
if you have one / your experience level] — is there flexibility on [base / sign-on / equity]?"
```

Rules:
- **Always express genuine enthusiasm first** — negotiating from a place of "I want this, let's
  make the numbers work" lands better than an adversarial tone.
- **Negotiate the whole package**, not just base — sign-on bonus and equity often have more
  flexibility than base salary bands.
- **Use a competing offer only if it's real** — never fabricate a competing offer; recruiters
  compare notes and getting caught costs you the offer and your reputation in that market.

## Handling notice period questions

```markdown
"My current notice period is [X weeks]. I can typically expedite that in coordination with my
current manager if there's a hard start-date constraint on your end — happy to discuss timelines
once we're further along."
```

Rules:
- **Be honest about your actual contractual notice period** — don't promise a shorter timeline
  you can't deliver; this becomes your problem to solve later, not theirs.
- If you have unused leave or a way to shorten notice legitimately, mention it as a possibility,
  not a promise, until you've confirmed it with your current employer.

## Rules for the whole conversation

- Get everything **in writing** once you're negotiating specifics — verbal agreements from a
  recruiter aren't binding until the written offer reflects them.
- Don't negotiate over text/chat for anything substantive — ask for a call, it reads as more
  serious and avoids misread tone.
