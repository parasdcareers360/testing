# "Tell Me About Yourself" Template

> **Type:** Template — copy or fill in directly

## Why interviewers ask this

It's a warm-up, but also the first data point on communication skills — a rambling, unstructured
answer sets a weak tone for the rest of the loop, even in purely technical rounds. Keep it tight
and rehearsed.

## Structure (60-90 seconds total)

```markdown
**1. Who you are now (10-15 sec)**
<Current role, years of experience, core stack — one sentence.>

**2. The through-line of your career (20-30 sec)**
<What kind of problems you've gravitated toward — pick a genuine theme, e.g. "systems where
correctness and performance both matter" — don't just list job titles chronologically.>

**3. One or two concrete highlights (20-30 sec)**
<Your strongest 1-2 accomplishments, briefly — full depth comes later if asked, this is a
trailer, not the movie.>

**4. Why you're here / what you're looking for now (10-15 sec)**
<Connects to why_this_company_template.md — brief, don't repeat that whole answer here.>
```

## Rules

- **Not a chronological resume readout.** "I started at X doing Y, then moved to Z..." is the
  single most common way this answer goes wrong — it's boring and shows no synthesis.
- **No personal life details** unless directly relevant to a professional narrative (e.g. how you
  got into programming, if genuinely interesting) — keep it professional and on-topic.
- **Practice out loud with a timer.** This is the question every interview opens with; an
  unrehearsed answer here undermines credibility before any technical question is asked.

## Model answer (illustrative — replace with your own real facts)

> "I'm a backend engineer with three years of experience, mostly in Django and DRF, backed by
> PostgreSQL and Elasticsearch. The thread through my work has been systems where data volume and
> correctness both matter — I've spent a lot of time on search relevance, document ingestion
> pipelines including OCR processing, and more recently on the infrastructure question of how
> that kind of pipeline connects to LLM-based retrieval. The project I'm proudest of is a search
> latency redesign that cut p95 response time by over 80% for our highest-traffic endpoints. I'm
> looking for a role where I can go deeper on systems at real scale, which is what brought me to
> this conversation."
