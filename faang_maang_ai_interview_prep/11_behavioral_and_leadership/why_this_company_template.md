# "Why This Company" Template

> **Type:** Template — copy or fill in directly

## Why interviewers ask this

They're filtering out candidates applying everywhere with no real interest (higher attrition
risk) and checking you've done basic homework. A generic answer ("great culture, smart people,
big scale") is instantly recognizable as unprepared.

## Structure

```markdown
### <Company>

**1. Something specific and true about their product/engineering that you genuinely find
interesting** (not copied from their homepage verbatim — your actual technical read on it)
<e.g. "I've used <specific product/feature> and the way it handles <specific technical thing> is
the kind of problem I want to work on.">

**2. A connection to your own experience/interests** (why THIS company's problems specifically
suit what you're good at or want to grow into)
<e.g. "My background is in search relevance and document processing, which maps directly onto
<team/product area>.">

**3. (Optional, if true) a personal/values connection** — only include if genuine, skip if it'd
feel forced
```

## Rules

- **Research the actual team/product you're interviewing for**, not just the company brand —
  "why Amazon" is weaker than "why the team building X within Amazon" if you know which team.
- **Never say "the compensation" or "the brand name" as your stated reason**, even if partially
  true — save that for the actual offer-negotiation conversation, not this question.
- **Don't fabricate familiarity** — if asked a follow-up about a specific product detail you
  don't actually know, say so honestly rather than bluffing; interviewers can tell.
- Keep it to 60-90 seconds. This isn't a story, it's a pitch.

## Model answer (illustrative — replace facts with your own genuine research)

> "I've been following how [Company]'s approach to [specific, real technical area] has evolved —
> the shift toward [specific real technical pattern] is the kind of systems problem I want more
> of in my day-to-day, and it's a natural extension of the backend/search work I've been doing.
> I'm also specifically interested in [team/product area] because it overlaps directly with the
> document-processing and search-relevance work I described earlier."
