# Microsoft

> **Type:** Study notes

Interview process varies by role, team, level, location, and hiring cycle — the loop below is the
commonly reported *typical* shape for a mid-level (~3 YOE) SWE hire, not a guarantee.

## Interview stages

Typically: a recruiter screen, then a technical phone screen, then an onsite/virtual-onsite loop of
roughly 4-5 interviews (often compressed into a single day for virtual loops). The loop typically
mixes 2-3 coding rounds and one system/technical design round, plus what Microsoft commonly calls
an **"As Appropriate" (AA) round** — a conversation, often with a senior engineer or the hiring
manager, focused on culture fit and overall impression rather than a fixed technical rubric. Loops
at Microsoft are generally reported as somewhat less rigidly scripted than Amazon's or Google's.

## Coding expectations

- Real coding editor is the common setup, with the ability to run code.
- 2-3 dedicated coding rounds, moderate difficulty — Microsoft loops are widely reported as less
  purely LeetCode-grind-heavy than Amazon or Meta, with more room for a back-and-forth,
  discussion-driven approach to reaching a solution rather than a race against the clock.
- Bar-raising signal here specifically: collaborative problem solving — interviewers commonly
  engage as a thinking partner (hints given more readily than at some other companies), so how you
  incorporate a hint and keep moving matters as much as whether you needed one. Explaining your
  thinking clearly enough that a non-expert could follow is a commonly cited positive signal.

## System design expectations for 3 YOE

In scope for most mid-level loops, typically at moderate depth: a service boundary, data model, API
shape, and one or two scaling/reliability considerations. Microsoft's product surface area is huge
(Azure, Windows, Office/M365, Xbox, LinkedIn, GitHub, and more, each with different norms), so the
depth and flavor of the design round can vary more by org than at some tighter-loop companies —
expect the interviewer to calibrate depth to your stated experience rather than a fixed script.

## Behavioral expectations

Microsoft's publicly stated cultural pillars — most notably **"growth mindset"** (a phrase Microsoft
has explicitly built into its culture since the Nadella era) and customer obsession — show up in the
AA round and are commonly woven into other rounds too. Concretely: stories that show learning from
failure, adapting an approach based on feedback, and collaborating across a team (rather than
"lone hero" narratives) land well. Microsoft is also known for weighting how you talk about
disagreement — respectful, evidence-based pushback is viewed more favorably than either silent
compliance or unproductive conflict.

## Common evaluation criteria

Because the loop is somewhat less rigidly structured than Amazon's or Google's, the AA round /
hiring manager conversation carries outsized weight in the final call — a strong technical
performance paired with a flat or awkward AA round is a commonly cited reason loops don't convert.
Consistency of "would I want to work with this person day to day" across the panel matters as much
as any single technical answer.

## How prep should differ for this company

- Practice thinking out loud in a genuinely collaborative style — pause to invite the interviewer's
  input rather than treating the round as a solo monologue; this maps to how Microsoft interviewers
  commonly engage.
- Prepare a strong "growth mindset" story (a time you were wrong, got feedback, and visibly changed
  approach) for the AA round, in addition to standard behavioral stories in
  [`11_behavioral_and_leadership/`](../11_behavioral_and_leadership/).
- Use [`06_system_design/fundamentals/`](../06_system_design/fundamentals/) at moderate depth, and
  be ready to adapt your design's flavor if the interviewer signals a specific org's priorities
  (e.g., Azure-style scale vs. a product team's user-facing latency concerns).
- Don't over-optimize for interview-only LeetCode speed drills at the expense of being able to
  explain trade-offs conversationally — Microsoft loops reward the latter more than some peers do.

## Company-specific interview checklist

- [ ] Practiced narrating thought process in a way a non-specialist could follow along with
- [ ] Comfortable accepting and building on interviewer hints without losing composure
- [ ] Have a dedicated "growth mindset" (learned from failure/feedback) story ready for the AA round
- [ ] Prepared at least one story on respectful, evidence-based disagreement with a teammate/manager
- [ ] System design prep at moderate depth, adaptable to different Microsoft org flavors
- [ ] Researched which Microsoft org/team you're interviewing for and adjusted talking points accordingly
- [ ] Rehearsed a collaborative (not monologue) coding style out loud, not just silently
- [ ] Reviewed customer-obsession framing and can tie at least one story to a real user/customer outcome
